<h1>The Stacks</h1>

<p class="codex-meta">10,512 books <span class="stack-sep" aria-hidden="true">·</span> counted nightly <span class="stack-sep" aria-hidden="true">·</span> holes admitted</p>

<div markdown="1" class="dropcap">
The canon records the favourites; this page counts the whole shelf. 10,512 books, the lot, audited by a machine and annotated by the man who fed it. The canon explains what the library means; this page is what the library is, down to the holes in it. The numbers recompute nightly and the sentences stay the same, which is the honest division of labour between me and the thing I built.
</div>

## I. The Build Year
{: #the-build-year}

The whole collection entered the shelves in one year, and the machine watched it happen. 2026-10 was the loudest month at 1,964 books; even the quietest, 2026-11, added 274. A library assembled this fast is not inherited. It is built, and the curve below is the construction record.

The treemap below is the same partition redrawn so the area of every block is the true size of the branch. ## III. The Classes
{: #the-classes}

The tree above is how I file the shelf; this is how the Library of Congress would. 4,371 books carry an LCC mark, spread across all twenty-one main classes, and the chart reads the first letter of every call number. P, Language and Literature, leads at 2,188; the smallest class on the chart, V, Naval Science, still holds 2. The marks are the cataloguers', not my guesses. Another 1,425 carry Dewey decimals as well.

## VIII. Errata
{: #errata}

Every archive has holes; this one admits its own. Of 10,512 books on the shelf, 10,359 carry no rating: 99 percent, which is the shelf's way of saying the reading outpaces the judging. Another 1,081 have no ISBN on file. The catalogue answers to the census, not the reverse.
